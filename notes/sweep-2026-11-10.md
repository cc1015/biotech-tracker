## Patterns

- **Pharma is buying platforms, not just partnering with them.** AstraZeneca–Omniose follows the same path as argenx–UNP the day before: years of exclusive collaboration, then an outright buy of the discovery engine. Omniose is a small WashU/UGA-founded platform company, and the price wasn't disclosed, which is typical of bolt-on platform buys.
- **Degraders are moving into neuro.** Oyster's $60M is for a brain-penetrant oral tau degrader, and Lilly joined as a new investor. Oyster has a hybrid platform-plus-lead-asset model with UK venture backing (Syncona, OSE). Its academic origin isn't confirmed. Hot silos are `protein-degradation` and `small-molecule`.
- **Chinese AI biologics companies are signing big deals fast, now with VCs as partners.** Funnel was founded in February 2026, and nine months later it signed a deal headlined at $2.16B. The counterparty is Third Rock, a venture studio, not a pharma, which suggests Third Rock will source antibodies for its own new companies. Silos: `ai-discovery` and `antibody`.
- **Policy and BD drive this week's stock moves.** Alteogen's fall came from US pricing policy (CMS treats SC reformulations as the same drug), not from data. BioNTech's $205M voucher purchase shows PRVs still selling above $150M, and Scholar Rock gets non-dilutive cash right after its first launch.
- **Asset-buyer launches:** IMVIONIX was formed by the former Alimera team with activist-fund backing (Caligan, Velan). It launched by buying a mature brand that big pharma is shedding, which is a commercial-stage, single-asset way to build a company.
- **Tech entrants:** no new Alphabet/Isomorphic, NVIDIA, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta, Apple, OpenAI or Anthropic life-sciences moves in the window. The Isomorphic ~$5B raise is still only talks. Anthropic's IPO is calendared for 11-12.
