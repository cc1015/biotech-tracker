## Patterns
- **AI companies are now running drug programs, not just selling tools.** Anthropic bought a one-person antibody biotech and is running its programs in-house, a week after OpenAI bought Orchestra Bio (10-20) and two days after Anthropic's S-1. Its partner list mixes pharma (BMS), Flagship's Generate, AI biotechs (BigHat, Divergence, Enveda) and a VC, The Column Group, which is forming a new company (Pleiades) to run Claude-driven programs. The "Pipeline-in-a-Person" pitch (one scientist, Claude, and contract labs) is a new way to build a company: virtual, asset-centric, with almost no headcount.
- **AI money is moving into clinical development.** Atlas Bio (founder-led; ex-Google engineers; Basel) sells simulated virtual-patient cohorts aimed at replacing Phase 2 and animal work. Formation Bio is raising a $600M fund to in-license stalled assets. Both bet that the bottleneck is the clinic, not molecule design, which matches Microsoft–Boehringer (10-22) and Pathos (10-23).
- **Antibodies and engineered biologics dominate the deals:** Astellas' IL-12 antibody-cytokine fusion (preclinical, a $285M upfront/option package), Genmab's bispecific Phase 3 win and Mithril's bispecifics. Asia-Pacific sourcing continues, here from Australia (Audax) rather than China.
- **Mid-size pharma is buying cell-based platforms in niche areas:** Ferring–Gameto (iPSC-derived ovarian support cells; academic-adjacent founding; one asset, Fertilo, in Phase 3) is women's-health M&A outside the big-15 families.
- **Commercial-stage readouts are moving stocks.** Kiniksa and Zura moved ≥15% on Q3 updates, not trial data. The big-pharma Phase 3 (retatrutide) barely moved LLY (+0.46%).
