## Patterns

- **The IPO window is open for clinical-stage I&I and neuro/cell therapy.** Bambusa (upsized, priced at the midpoint) and Aspen (priced at the low end) take 2026 to 29 US biotech IPOs (BioPharma Dive). That follows Lycia ($317.5M) and Corxel's SPAC last week. Late-stage private rounds are getting bigger too: Dren's $350M is a pre-launch crossover-style round.
- **Spinouts from acquisitions are a distinct way companies get built.** Setara is the leftover pipeline of Actio after Jazz bought it, with the acquirer holding a stake (like the Cellarity SCD carve-out in yesterday's Etcelera merger). Bambusa is another in-licensing build: ex-BioNTech executives licensing bispecifics from BioNTech's Chinese unit Biotheus. Both are asset companies, not platforms.
- **Hot silos:** antibodies/bispecifics (Dren, Bambusa) and cell therapy (Aspen's iPSC therapy; Castle Creek's gene-modified fibroblasts; the Iovance TIL sell-off). RNA came under market pressure: Alnylam lost a quarter of its value despite +52% sales.
- **Earnings-driven stock moves dominated** the market tape (Lilly, Glaukos, Bausch, Alnylam, Replimune yesterday). The tracker's 15% / 5% thresholds are catching quarterly-result reactions more than clinical news this week.
- **Tech entrants:** Google is building regional health-AI capacity (Singapore hub with NUS/NTU, DeepMind's co-clinician with NUHS) rather than drug discovery. This week's drug-discovery news came from Anthropic (Mithril) and Flagship (Etcelera).
