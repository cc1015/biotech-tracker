## Patterns
- **RNA beyond the liver is the hottest theme.** City Therapeutics' $200M IPO, Merck KGaA's $1.3B purchase of Silence, Roche–Rona ALK7 RNAi (up to $1.16B), Lilly–METiS AI-designed LNPs (~$1.1B), Atrium's $50M PIPE and Undecimal's $18M seed are all about extrahepatic RNA delivery. Small molecules still lead on count (11 events), mostly single-asset raises (KymaThera, RougeTx, Altay, TriGemX).
- **Pharma is buying platforms through licensing, not acquisitions.** Roche signed three deals in two weeks (Hansoh ADCs at $400M upfront and up to $15B+, Rona, Defand glues), plus Genentech–Alector. Other platform deals: Lilly–Gate, Pfizer–Enodia, GSK–Chai and CSL–Alentis. The M&A is mostly small or tools/diagnostics (Agilent–Proscia, Hologic–Reveal Genomics, Labcorp–BioReference).
- **Alternative routes to going public are back.** There were two SPACs (TriGemX/TCGX with $246M, ArcheNova/Horizon Space at $2B) and two reverse-merger-plus-PIPE deals (Alaunos–Velaris $350M, Recursion's $200M follow-on). Conventional IPOs (City, TRex, Retension, Iambic) also kept going.
- **How companies are built:** of the 25 new company rows, most are founder-led (11) or unknown (8). There are 5 academic spinouts (Estuary from Yale, TriGemX/Kallyope from Columbia, RougeTx, Alentis) and only 2 studio/incubator builds (Velaris from Paragon Labs, Enodia from Argobio). None came from Flagship, ARCH, Third Rock, Atlas or Foresite this week. Platforms (15) outnumber single-asset companies (9).
- **Tech companies:** the main move in the window was Google DeepMind's RAISE program (AlphaFold 3 and AlphaGenome access plus $3M in compute for 200 EU researchers). NVIDIA showed up only as a technology supplier (IQVIA Life Science Models built on Nemotron) and as co-host of GenScript's PitchFest, which was rejected as a startup competition. Again nothing from Microsoft or Meta. Isomorphic's reported $40B+ funding talks haven't closed.
- **Industrial biotech is getting government money:** Antheia's $80M DoW Office of Strategic Capital loan, plus Prolific Machines and Sensible (In-Q-Tel; Slovak and EU grants).
