## Patterns

- **Pharma keeps buying single assets early.** J&J–Ollin is the third deal in two months where an in-licensed Chinese bispecific becomes a VC-built US company and is then bought. Ollin went from founding to a $3.3B exit in about three years. Lilly–Calytrix is earlier still: a preclinical mutant-CALR antibody from a 2025 company, and Lilly's second MPN buy after Ajax. Both are asset-centric companies, not platforms.
- **Risk-sharing structures are spreading.** Blackstone Life Sciences will pay J&J $750M to fund Ollin's Phase 3 in return for milestones and royalties. This follows Blackstone's $1.5B berobenatide deal with Pfizer the week before. Private-credit-style R&D funding is becoming a regular part of large deals.
- **Non-viral DNA delivery is pulling in pharma money early.** Seawolf, a Versant-founded company that looks studio-built (founders aren't named), launched with $100M. Its strategic investors include Lilly, the Novartis Venture Fund and CRISPR Therapeutics, and it has a Sanofi license worth up to $1.2B before it even has a development candidate. It is a platform company whose partnership came at launch rather than later.
- **The FDA's OS bar is hitting the China-licensing trade.** The ivonescimab CRL sent Akeso down 22% in Hong Kong and dragged on other HK biotech names. Summit fell about 20% premarket. Jefferies called the CRL the base case.
- **Small Asian academic spinouts:** Liflex, an SNU spinout with a microRNA-degradation platform, closed a modest Series A. Korean VC rounds remain small (around $5M) compared with US launches.
- **Tech companies:** no new moves in the window. Anthropic's IPO is still expected around 11-19.
