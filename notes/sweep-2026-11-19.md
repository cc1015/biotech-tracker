## Patterns

- **AI drug discovery is now raising money at Big Tech scale.** Isomorphic's $5B Series C, co-led by Greenoaks and Sequoia with Alphabet still investing, is the largest private biotech round on record. It brings the company to about $7.7B raised in 18 months, and it still has no disclosed clinical asset. Two days later it added MSD as its fourth big-pharma partner, after Lilly, Novartis and J&J. That deal covers a single target with undisclosed economics. Boltz (an MIT spinout from the Barzilay lab) raised $110M for open-source models plus paid usage. The AI layer is splitting into a vertically integrated drug developer (Isomorphic) and a tools company that sells the models (Boltz).
- **Hot silos: RNA/antibody conjugates, complement and ADC platforms.** Marrow ($173M; studio-built by Scion) is a conjugate-delivery platform that targets bone-marrow TfR1 as a redosable alternative to ex vivo gene editing for sickle cell. Commit (an Aarhus spinout) uses complement instead of T cells to kill target cells. Platform licensing for ADCs is also heating up: Lilly–Stipple (up to $5.9B, before Stipple has entered the clinic) and Novartis–OBI (>$2B for conjugation technology rather than a specific drug).
- **Pharma money is arriving at seed and Series A.** Commit's seed includes AbbVie Ventures and M Ventures. Lilly is taking equity in Stipple, and last sweep it joined Seawolf's Series A. Lilly did two deals on 11-16 alone (Calytrix and Stipple), and both are preclinical.
- **How these companies were built:**
  - **Platforms:** Boltz, Commit, Marrow and Stipple; the majority of this sweep's raises.
  - **Asset-centric:** Aytu and Egetis (both commercial-stage) and Onward (in-licensed assets).
  - **Origins:** academic spinouts (MIT, Aarhus, UCSF/Fred Hutch) outnumber studio-built ones; Scion's Marrow is the only studio launch.
- **Regulatory:** two new drugs from big pharma were approved on the same day: AZ's Klygefa (a weekly self-injected C5 nanobody for gMG) and Roche's Wilserdly (an oral SERD for ESR1-mutated breast cancer, approved about 4 weeks early). Summit's ivonescimab CRL kept weighing on its stock: about −16.5% on 11-16 and a further ~10% down on 11-17.
- **Tech companies:** only Alphabet, through Isomorphic. No new life-sciences moves from NVIDIA, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta, OpenAI, Anthropic or Apple in the window. Anthropic's IPO, expected around 11-19, was not checked and is not a life-sciences event.
